Using the app

Adding, moving and removing scales
  1. + Spring scale adds a scale, up to six. You can also click an empty hole on the bar.
  2. Change a scale's hole, force and angle with its sliders or by typing a number.
  3. Drag the handle at the end of a scale's arrow to change its force and direction together.
  4. The × next to a scale removes it. Setting a force to 0 N leaves the scale hooked in but pulling with no force.
Reading the angle

Angles are measured counterclockwise, seen from above, starting from "along the bar to the right." 90° pulls toward the top of the screen, −90° toward the bottom, and 0° or 180° pull along the bar.

How the apparatus works

You are looking down on an aluminum bar that lies flat in a horizontal plane and turns about a vertical axle through its center. Each spring scale hooks into a hole and pulls in any direction across the table. Readings are taken with the bar held still in its starting position. The bar turns toward whichever turning effect wins and stays still only when they cancel.

Because the bar turns in a horizontal plane, gravity pulls along the axle and has no turning effect.

Let go of the bar lets it turn freely from rest, with each pull keeping its angle to the bar. The app treats the bar as a uniform 200 g rod with no friction at the axle and stops it after two turns or four seconds. A real bar has some friction.

The physics (Analyze mode)

Torque about the pivot from one force: τ = r F sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the hole, F is the force, and θ is the angle between the lever arm (pivot → hole) and the force. Only F sin θ, the part of the force perpendicular to the bar, turns it.

Counterclockwise is positive. The bar is in rotational equilibrium when Στ = 0.

Angles: φ is the direction of the pull, measured counterclockwise (seen from above) from the direction along the bar to the right. 90° points toward the top of the screen and −90° toward the bottom; both are straight across the bar.
