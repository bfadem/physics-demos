Shorthand to Math

Type equations in simple shorthand, one per line. See them typeset, then copy them into the Canvas equation editor.

Putting an equation into a Canvas answer

  1. Click Copy LaTeX next to the equation you want.
  2. In the Canvas answer box, click the Insert Math Equation button (the √x icon in the toolbar).
  3. Turn on Directly Edit LaTeX, paste, and click Done or Insert.
  4. Type your explanation in ordinary words between equations. Do one equation at a time.

You don't have to typeset anything: clear shorthand typed directly in the answer box is fine for grading. This page is for when you'd like your work to look like real math.

What this page does, and doesn't do

It only changes how your equations look. It doesn't check your physics, fix mistakes, or fill in steps. Nothing you type leaves your browser. The derivation has to be your own work.

Spaces separate symbols: type m g, not mg (which also works but may look like one name). Put groups in parentheses: v_(Ax), 10^(-26), (a+b)/(c+d). Subscript words of three or more letters (net, avg, sys, ext, Earth) automatically come out upright.
