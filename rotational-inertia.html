Using the app

Moving the masses
  1. Drag a mass onto any hole on the bar. It bolts into the nearest hole.
  2. Drag a mass into the tray on the right to take it off the bar.
  3. The presets put both masses in the holes closest to the rod, in the holes farthest out, or in the tray.
The twist

The red arrow at the top left is your fingers on the rod. The twist is always the same size: one way for half a second, then the other way, over and over. Nothing on the page can change how hard you twist.

How the apparatus is modeled

You are looking down the rod. A light aluminum bar (32 cm long, 60 g) is screwed to the end of the rod at its center and has six holes on each side, 2.5 cm apart. Each mass is a round 200 g slug (unless you choose another size) bolted into a hole. There is no friction, so the only thing that decides how the bar responds is how its mass is spread out around the rod.

Your twist is a torque of 0.013 N·m that reverses every half second. The first push lasts a quarter second, so the bar swings between its starting line and the far edge of the shaded wedge instead of drifting off to one side.

The physics (for the curious)

For a twist that switches direction every h seconds, the bar swings through an angle of τ h² / (4 I), where I is the rotational inertia. The same twist gives a smaller swing when I is larger.

I adds up the rod, the bar (M L²/12) and each mass (m r², plus a small amount for the slug's own size), where r is the distance from the rod to the mass.

These sizes are typical values, not measurements of a particular lab's equipment.
